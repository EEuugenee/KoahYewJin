Building design / Academic project

Tianjin University / Dec 2024 - Jun 2025

From load paths to reinforcement details.

Structural design of a 10-storey reinforced-concrete office building

I developed the structural layout and load paths from architectural drawings, selecting an RC moment-frame system. The project connected whole-building analysis with the design of individual members and connections.

My contribution

I cross-checked hand-calculated frame designs against PKPM results and used SATWE for full-building 3D analysis. I designed beam and column reinforcement at governing sections to GB 50010, including serviceability checks and reinforcement detailing.

I also checked the seismic detailing of beam-column joint core regions against ductility requirements.

Design workflow

  1. 01

    Establish the structural system

    Translate architectural drawings into an RC frame layout and identify the load paths.

  2. 02

    Cross-check the analysis

    Compare hand calculations with PKPM and assess global behaviour in SATWE.

  3. 03

    Design and detail the members

    Size reinforcement at governing sections and check serviceability and joint detailing.

01 / Design & problem solving

2nd place, Tianjin University

Protect the load path.

Tianjin University Structural Design Competition / Mar - May 2023

Our team designed and built a two-span, four-lane bamboo bridge for moving vehicle loads and a lateral pier impact. The challenge was to make a light, stable structure that also fitted the testing rig.

My contribution

I coordinated task allocation and progress, using AutoCAD and MIDAS Civil to support the design, global stability and load-path analysis. Our team built and load-tested the bamboo/timber prototype to compare predicted capacity with measured behaviour, addressing fit-up, weight and impact resistance together.

Impact test
560 g steel ball
Deflection limit
±15 mm
Deck width
At least 320 mm
Engineering decisions
  • Fit-up: adjusted the boundary interface to fit the rig while keeping the primary members continuous.
  • Weight: developed built-up T-sections and bracing to use the bamboo more efficiently.
  • Impact: used the corbel and upper deck together to carry vertical loads and distribute lateral demand.

  2. Review the other team's arrangement and raise questions.
  3. Resolve unclear steps and confirm the sequence together.
  4. Assemble and dismantle with shared responsibility.

03 / Site experience & communication

Connection fit-up case study

Making the mechanics clear.

A 30 mm gap at a balcony bracket support

An uneven wall left a gap behind the support plate. Using thick shims to bridge that gap would introduce an additional stand-off distance, changing the connection's eccentricity and the demand on its anchors.

My contribution

I raised the issue with the site team and explained the lever-arm effect using a simple comparison: a bucket held away from the body creates more turning demand than the same bucket held close. I escalated the detail for engineering review.

Outcome Work paused for review and an approved correction before continuing.

Connection context

The original detail used a 175 × 330 × 6 mm mild-steel plate with four M16 chemical anchors and 125 mm embedment.

Possible remedies included controlled correction of local high spots or an engineered packing detail, subject to substrate checks and approval by the structural engineer.

Dimensions describe this project case; they are not a general connection design.

02 / Research

Seismic performance of a 20-storey modular building.

Modular Integrated Construction (MiC)
The University of Hong Kong / Nov 2025 - Jul 2026

Building
20 storeys
Modelling tools
ETABS / Abaqus
Connection focus
Semi-rigid behaviour

How do inter-module connections affect the seismic response of the whole building?

I developed 3D finite element models in ETABS and Abaqus to study seismic load paths, lateral force resistance and dynamic response. I investigated connection stiffness and its influence on lateral load transfer and global ductility.

Analysis methods

  • Modal analysis
  • Nonlinear static pushover analysis
  • Time-history analysis

Research contribution

I developed a modified fundamental-period prediction method from modal analysis results and compared its predictions with Eurocode empirical formulas for semi-rigid modular structures.

  • Inter-module connection stiffness
  • Global ductility
  • Fundamental period
